Invited speaker for the Quantum Matter seminar in the School of Physics at the University of Bristol, UK.

<h2>Abstract</h2>
Accurately simulating magnetic materials remains a challenge for materials theorists. Magnetic moments represent an additional set of degrees of freedom which thermally disorder with increasing temperature. The resulting ensemble of magnetic configurations determines both a material’s underlying electronic structure and many consequent physical properties that experiments probe. These magnetic moments also couple to atomic degrees of freedom, meaning that a material’s magnetic state can influence its phase stability and vice versa. In this talk, from the perspective of first-principles electronic structure calculations and atomistic simulation, I will present a selection of results from recent investigations in this area. I will describe: (i) how magnetic order drives spinodal decomposition in ternary Cu–Ni–Fe alloys [1], (ii) how thermally induced spin fluctuations cause altermagnetic band splitting in CrSb and MnTe to collapse rapidly with increasing temperature [2], and (iii) how machine learning, specifically magnetism-aware machine-learned interatomic potentials, can accelerate otherwise computationally demanding atomic-scale modelling of the mechanical and defect properties of materials containing magnetic elements, such as austenitic stainless steels [3]. I will conclude by giving an outlook on the work I intend to conduct in my new position at Cambridge, where my Fellowship focuses on improving the treatment of magnetism in these machine-learning models.
