Contributed talk at the 2026 American Physical Society (APS) Global Physics Summit in Denver, Colorado, USA.

<h2>Abstract</h2>
So called ‘altermagnets’ are an intriguing class of magnetically ordered material in which there is a net zero magnetisation, but in which electronic bands are not spin-degenerate on account of the intrinsic symmetries of the underlying crystal structure. In the zero-temperature limit, the magnetic moments (or ‘spins’) will arranged in a perfectly ordered, collinear structure. However, at any finite temperature, thermal noise will cause the spins to deviate from this pristine arrangement. Above some critical temperature (the Néel temperature), long-range magnetic order disappears entirely, and the material transitions to a paramagnetic state. Here, we will present results of ab initio electronic structure calculations performed on two prototypical altermagnets: CrSb, which is metallic; and MnTe, which is semiconducting. We demonstrate that the magnetic phase transition in both materials is well-described by disordered local moment (DLM) theory [1-3] and describe in detail how site-diagonal spin disorder induces changes to calculated band structures. We provide preliminary estimates for the Néel temperature of both materials, and also discuss implications for temperature-dependent transport properties.
