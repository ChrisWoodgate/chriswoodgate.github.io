I am always happy to hear from people interested in working with me! See below for details of collaborative opportunities and a note on my current status with regard to my ability to support postdocs, PhD students, and funding applications.

Collaboration
------

Check out my [research](/research/), [software](/software/), and [publications](/publications/) pages to get a flavour of the sort of work I do and classes of physical systems I study. At the moment, I am excited to hear about opportunities to:
* Work on joint projects where my computational tools and methodological know-how would be useful.
* Work on implementing new computational methods and features in software packages.
* Work with experimental groups, using my methods to interpret, explain, and guide experimental investigations.

Postdoctoral Positions and MSc/PhD Opportunities
------

In my current fixed-term contract as an independent postdoctoral research fellow, I unfortunately do not have the ability to recruit postdocs, or to formally supervise MSc and PhD students. As and when this situation changes, I will update this page. (Note also that I may therefore choose not to reply to emails contacting me about such opportunities.)

Funding Applications
------

For broadly the same reasons as those outlined above, I am unable to act as a primary host for applications to funding schemes such as postdoctoral fellowships. I am, however, generally happy to provide advice and direct interested individuals to relevant colleagues who may be able to support such applications. Again, as and when this situation changes, I will update this page.

Get in Touch
------

If interested in working with me, you can get in touch via [email](mailto:christopher.woodgate@physics.org). It would be helpful if you could concisely explain your background and what you would be interested in getting out of working with me. In this age of large language models (LLMs) and agentic artificial intelligence (AI), I also particularly value emails written by _human beings_; it is usually exceedingly obvious when someone has used AI to help them draft an email.
